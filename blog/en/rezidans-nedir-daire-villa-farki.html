What Is a Residence? How It Differs From Apartments and Villas

A residence blends the convenience of an apartment with the comfort of a villa, organized around service and security. This guide compares it clearly against apartments and villas and explains its lifestyle and investment value.

9 min read 2026-06-07

What Is a Residence? A Short, Clear Definition

A residence is a high-standard housing type in which individual units (apartments) are integrated with shared social areas, professional management and continuous services. The shortest answer to the question of what is a residence is this: you are not only buying the square meters you live in, but the whole building and the services it provides. Here, a home is not a standalone box; it is part of an ecosystem supported by a pool, security, landscaping and common areas.

In a classic apartment, your ownership ends at your four walls. In a residence, a planned service layer extends beyond those walls. Around-the-clock security, regular maintenance, cleaning of common areas and, in most projects, a swimming pool are defined as an inseparable part of the property. This is why residences usually sit in the luxury residence category and stand apart from standard housing stock.

The concept of the residence matured in the Turkish market over the past two decades. Once identified only with high-rise towers in major city centers, it now extends to coastal regions such as Didim Altinkum, in low-rise or mid-rise projects blended with modern architecture, wide balconies and sea views. In short, a residence does not have to mean a skyscraper; the defining feature is that service and quality of life are standardized.

Core Differences Between a Residence, an Apartment and a Villa

The line separating these three housing types is drawn not by ownership but by service and lifestyle. The residence versus apartment difference shows up most clearly in common areas and management: a standard apartment runs on its own, while a residence operates as a whole through a professional management team. The residence versus villa difference is read through privacy and scale; a villa gives you a large private garden and full independence, whereas a residence offers shared but high-quality amenities and a much lighter maintenance burden.

For a practical comparison, these distinctions are a useful guide:

  • Service: Almost none in an apartment; security, maintenance, landscaping and often a pool included in a residence; in a villa you mostly organize services yourself.
  • Security: A caretaker or simple entry in an apartment; controlled entry and cameras around the clock in a residence; independent and personal responsibility in a villa.
  • Common charges: Low in an apartment; higher but predictable in a residence depending on the service scope; no fixed charge in a villa, with scattered and variable costs.
  • Maintenance burden: Lowest in a residence because common areas are handled by management; highest in a villa.
  • Rental yield and liquidity: Well-located residences see strong demand for both holiday and annual rentals and find buyers faster on resale.

To sum up the table: an apartment represents economy and simplicity, a villa represents privacy and space, and a residence represents the balance between comfort and convenience. Especially for holiday or investment purchases, a residence is favored because it offers a quality of life close to a villa without the villa's maintenance headaches.

The Features That Define a Residence

For a project to truly be called a residence, certain residence features must come together. Some projects carry the 'residence' label for marketing reasons without offering the core services; this is why buyers should know the checklist. In a genuine residence, the shared living infrastructure comes first, and the qualities of the unit itself second.

The core features are usually these:

  • Swimming pool and social areas: A pool and relaxation areas open to residents and maintained by management.
  • Security infrastructure: Controlled entry, a camera system and, in most projects, continuous security staff.
  • Professional landscaping: Aesthetic, value-adding surroundings integrated with palm-lined, well-kept green areas.
  • Modern architecture and quality interiors: Units with wide, curved balconies, airy living rooms, quality materials and well-planned natural light.
  • Premium unit types: Special options such as sea-view upper floors and rooftop-terrace apartments that lift the project's overall value.

The point here is not to praise a brand, but to anchor an abstract definition to concrete, visible elements.

The Lifestyle and Comfort Advantages of a Residence

The advantages of a residence are felt most concretely in daily life. The biggest gain is time and peace of mind: pool maintenance, garden watering, cleaning of common areas and providing security all leave your agenda. This management layer is invaluable especially for a home used as a holiday property, since it stays empty for much of the year; when you return, you find everything tidy and well-kept.

The second advantage is a sense of security. Controlled entry and continuous monitoring give peace both when you are at home and when you are away for months. For foreign buyers and for those who live elsewhere and own a holiday home, this is often the decisive factor in the purchase. Keeping an empty villa safe from a distance is hard, while in a professionally managed residence that burden is shared.

The third advantage is social and physical quality of life. A swimming pool, wide balconies and well-kept landscaping carry everyday life into a holiday atmosphere. Combined with a location just 50 meters from the sea, the home becomes not merely shelter but the promise of a lifestyle. Forming neighborly relations within a more predictable community of similar expectations is, for most residents, an added comfort.

Investment Value and Rental Yield

Residences usually trade at a higher price per square meter than standard apartments in the same area; yet that premium often returns through stronger value retention and faster liquidity. In coastal areas, when location and quality come together, a well-planned residence becomes the first choice of both buyers and tenants. In the luxury residence segment, demand tends to be more resilient to economic swings than standard stock.

In terms of rental yield, residences earn through two channels. The first is daily and weekly holiday rental in the summer months; a pool, security and proximity to the sea directly raise the daily rate. The second is steady, stable income from annual rental. An investor can choose one of these models according to seasonality and occupancy goals, or combine both across the year.

Several practical points deserve attention in valuation: distance to the sea, the view, the floor and unit type (rooftop-terrace units, for example, often command a premium), the quality of site management, and whether the common charges are proportionate to the service scope. When assessing the investment, the healthiest approach is to look not only at the purchase price but at the expected net rental yield, the occupancy rate and the ease of resale together.

Common Charges and Running Costs: What to Know

Common charges in a residence work on a different logic from the classic 'fuel and elevator' fee of an apartment. The charge covers the cost of continuous services such as pool maintenance, security staff, common-area cleaning, landscaping and general management. This is why a residence charge is higher than a standard apartment's; but since you receive tangible service in return, it is less an expense than the price of the living standard you have bought.

The right comparison is not to judge the charge alone, but to read it together with its scope. A villa has no fixed charge, yet you organize pool maintenance, a gardener, security and repairs separately yourself; once those items are added up, they often approach or exceed a residence's charge, and on top of that you carry the management burden. In other words, the charge in a residence turns scattered costs into a single, predictable line.

The practical questions to ask before buying are these: Exactly which services are included in the charge? Are the pool and common areas operated year-round or seasonally? Is management run by a professional firm? What has the charge's increase history looked like over time? Clear answers to these questions directly affect both your living comfort and your investment return; it is wise to approach projects that leave the answers vague with caution.

Common Mistakes When Buying a Residence

The first and most frequent mistake is to look only at the unit and fail to evaluate the project as a whole. The value of a residence is set by its common areas, management quality and service continuity; a buyer fixated only on the unit's square meters and price misses what they are really buying. It is essential to see on site whether the pool is actually operated, whether security is continuous, and whether the landscaping is kept well-maintained.

The second mistake is not researching the track record of the developer and the management. The experience of the firm that built the project, its delivery quality and its post-handover management model directly affect the home's value ten years later. A well-built but poorly managed residence loses value over time; this is why operating quality must be questioned as much as construction quality. Here, working with firms that know the region, such as Danis Insaat, which developed the project in Didim Altinkum, gives buyers confidence in delivery and management continuity.

Other frequent mistakes include: proceeding without clarifying the title deed and occupancy permit status; not confirming the scope of common charges in writing; basing rental-yield expectations on the seller's promise rather than realistic market data; and underrating elements that permanently affect value, such as the view, the floor and distance to the sea. Every one of these mistakes is avoidable; all it takes is balancing an emotional decision with concrete checklists.

Who Is a Residence the Right Choice For?

A residence fits certain needs and lifestyles very well. It is the most sensible choice for buyers who want a comfortable holiday or second home without the maintenance hassle, because the property is professionally managed during the months spent away. For those dreaming of a peaceful retirement by the coast, security, social areas and a low maintenance burden form an ideal combination.

For investors, a residence is a strong instrument because it offers both value retention and dual-channel rental income. For foreign buyers, the managed structure of a residence largely overcomes language and distance barriers; knowing the property stays safe and well-kept, even from afar, makes the purchase decision easier. Regions such as Didim Altinkum are especially suitable for those who prefer not a crowded city-center tower but a quality, human-scale project by the sea.

By contrast, a villa may be more appropriate for those who prioritize a very large private garden, full privacy and unlimited freedom over the property. When deciding, it is enough to ask yourself one question: do you want to take on the maintenance burden, or buy comfort and gain time? The answer clarifies the right housing type for most buyers. Throughout this process, speaking with a team that knows the region and the product well is the fastest way to identify the right unit and the right expectations.

Key takeaways

  • A residence combines the convenience of an apartment with the comfort of a villa around service and security; you buy not just square meters but a managed living ecosystem.
  • Its difference from an apartment is common areas and professional management; from a villa, a low maintenance burden and shared but high-quality amenities.
  • A pool, around-the-clock security, professional landscaping, modern architecture and premium unit types such as rooftop terraces define a genuine residence.
  • Common charges are higher than a standard apartment's but turn scattered costs into a single predictable line; confirm the scope in writing.
  • Well-located residences retain value, offer fast liquidity and provide dual-channel income from both holiday and annual rental.
  • For the right decision, look not at the unit but at the whole project, the developer's track record and the management quality.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the biggest difference between a residence and a standard apartment?
The biggest difference is service and management. In a standard apartment your ownership ends at your four walls and shared services are minimal. In a residence, continuous services such as around-the-clock security, a pool, landscaping and common-area maintenance are provided through professional management; you buy not just the unit but a whole living standard.
Why are residence common charges higher, and are they worth it?
Charges are higher because they cover the cost of continuous services such as pool maintenance, security staff, common-area cleaning and landscaping. When you organize these services separately in a villa, the total cost is often similar or higher, and you also carry the management burden. So residence charges are usually worth it because they turn scattered costs into a single predictable line.
Does a residence make sense as an investment, and how is the rental yield?
Well-located residences usually retain value well and find buyers faster on resale. Rental yield comes from two channels: daily and weekly holiday rentals in summer, and steady annual rental year-round. Features such as a pool, security and proximity to the sea raise the rate. When assessing the investment, look beyond the purchase price at net rental yield, occupancy rate and ease of resale.
Why are residences preferred in coastal areas like Didim Altinkum?
In coastal areas homes can stay empty for much of the year, so professional management and security are critical; a residence takes on that burden for you. Proximity to the sea, a pool and well-kept landscaping carry daily life into a holiday atmosphere while removing the maintenance hassle. Modern projects 50 meters from the sea, such as Letoon Residence, are favored by holiday and investment buyers because they combine this comfort and security with coastal living.
